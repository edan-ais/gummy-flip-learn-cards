StudyLamb welcome guide (sample class)

This is the sample class that comes with StudyLamb. Instead of a school subject, it teaches you how to use the app itself, so every feature you try here works exactly the same in your own classes.

Chapter 1 · Getting started

Classes and exams. A class is one school subject, like Science or Math. You create it with a name and a color, and it can hold as many exams as you like, such as Unit 1 test and Final. Everything you can add, you can also rename or delete with the pencil and trash buttons.

Adding materials. Open an exam and add your study materials: PDFs, photos of pages, Word documents (.docx), plain text files, or pasted text, up to 25 MB each. The camera button opens your camera directly. StudyLamb reads the pages and builds chapters, flashcards, practice questions, and podcast episodes from your own material.

Syllabus upload. From the classes page, Upload a syllabus reads your syllabus and creates the classes and their major exams for you, with dates when listed.

Source of truth. Chapter material from the textbook or teacher outranks student notes. When they contradict, only the true version enters the study guide. Information repeated across many of your notes is treated as important and weighted more heavily.

Duplicates. Identical files are detected and skipped, so nothing is processed twice.

Final review. At the end of a semester, use Final review: pick classes on the classes page to checkbox the classes on your final and combine all their cards and questions into one free review exam.

Colors and wording. Blue screens are for learning, red for quizzing, green for correct answers. Misses are called "Not yet" with a next step, and feedback praises strategy and effort.

Chapter 2 · Study tools

Flashcards. Spaced repetition drives every page: what you know well returns later as review, what you struggle with returns sooner and more often. Tap a card to flip it and grade yourself honestly.

Flashcard audio. Turn audio on to hear Professor Lamby's podcast voice automatically on each card and flip. The current word is highlighted with a bouncing ball; word timing is approximate. New recordings cost BrainBucks, and cached replays are free.

Drawing. You can sketch on any study card; the ink is slightly transparent so the words stay readable.

Memorize. Type each word from its first letter, over three rounds. Passing requires accuracy strictly above 90%, and a neighboring keyboard key counts as a match. A wrong letter turns the word red immediately, shows the key you pressed, and reveals the correct word.

Listen tab. The first tab inside an exam. Q&A mode reads every question and answer in order; Abstract mode turns the topic into a fun, memorable audio lesson. The mini-player has a close button.

Home and notes. The exam Home page shows progress, daily goal, the due date box, sample exam, chapters, what you uploaded, and your notes. My notes holds typed or spoken notes; voice notes are transcribed for you.

Chapter 3 · Practice and extras

Practice and sample exams. Practice gives immediate feedback, weighted toward what you find hard. Sample exam lets you pick 5, 10, 20, or all questions, grade each answer Got it or Not yet, see your percentage, and retake only the questions you missed. Missed questions also surface more often in Cards and Practice.

Due dates and pacing. Set a Test, Exam, or Homework due date on Home. About three days out or less, cram mode teaches more in less time by leading with unknown cards. With more time, new cards are spread into small daily sets plus review. Timers are off by default.

BrainBucks. AI work costs BrainBucks (the dollar-bill chip): building a study set costs 5 plus 1 per PDF or photo, an episode costs 3, a new read-aloud clip or spoken note costs 1. Replays are free, and failed work is refunded. New students start with 25 free BrainBucks. A balance of 5 or fewer triggers a top-up reminder, and any action costing 95% or more of your balance shows a warning with cheaper options before you confirm. If you run out mid task, a 3 BrainBuck grace buffer lets the current step finish; beyond that the task pauses until you top up.

Settings. Optional supports include Dyslexia (structured literacy with explicit sound-letter patterns and the Word Lab), ADHD (short chunks, immediate feedback, visible progress, goal setting), read-aloud as a toggle, and adjustable text size and spacing.
